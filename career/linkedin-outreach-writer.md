# YOUR MISSION

Write LinkedIn messages that get replies from recruiters and hiring managers — without sounding like a bot, a template, or someone desperate for a job.

The failure mode you're preventing: sending 40 copy-paste messages that generate zero replies and make the sender look unprofessional. The success mode: a small number of personalized, relevant messages that start real conversations.

## Step 2 – Message Strategy

Before writing a single word, diagnose the situation:

### Why should they reply?

A recruiter or hiring manager has limited time and receives dozens of outreach messages weekly. They'll reply if:
1. I'm relevant to what they're actually working on right now
2. The message is short enough to read in 10 seconds
3. There's a clear, specific ask that's easy to say yes or no to
4. It doesn't feel like a template

Rate my outreach situation on each dimension:
- **Relevance signal**: How clearly can I connect my background to their current need? (Strong / Moderate / Weak)
- **Credibility signal**: Does my profile make me obviously credible for this type of role? (Strong / Moderate / Weak)
- **Warm vs. cold**: Do I have any connection point that reduces the "stranger" friction? (Yes / Borderline / None)

Adjust the strategy accordingly. A Weak/Weak/None situation needs a different approach than a Strong/Strong/Yes situation.

## Step 3 – The Message

### Core structure

Every message follows this logic (not necessarily this explicit structure):
1. **Context**: Who am I and why am I reaching out to them specifically (not "I found your profile")
2. **Relevance**: Why this conversation is worth their time (what I bring or what I'm looking for, in 1 sentence)
3. **Ask**: A specific, low-friction request (a 20-minute call, their thoughts on a role, a referral — not "I'm open to any opportunities")

## Step 5 – What Not to Write

For my specific situation, name the patterns I should avoid:

**The template tell**: phrases that signal I copied this from somewhere:
- "I came across your profile and was impressed by..."
- "I wanted to reach out regarding exciting opportunities..."
- "I am a highly motivated professional with X years of experience..."

**The desperation signal**: phrases that lower my perceived value:
- "I am open to any opportunities"
- "I would really appreciate any advice you could offer"
- Anything that mentions how hard my job search has been

**The wrong ask**: requests that create work for them:
- "Could you review my CV and let me know if you see any fit?"
- "Could you introduce me to the right person?" (without earning the right to ask)
- Asking for a referral in a cold first message

**The vague opener**: claims without evidence that waste their time:
- "I have extensive experience in product management" (every message says this)
- "I'm passionate about AI/fintech/SaaS" (so is everyone)

## Step 6 – Profile Audit (Optional)

If I'm doing outreach at volume, my profile matters as much as my message — because the first thing they'll do is click through.

Flag any mismatches between what my outreach promises and what my profile shows. Specifically:
- Headline: does it speak to the target audience, or is it a job title?
- About section: does it open with value, or with career timeline?
- Featured section: is there anything that makes me credible for the roles I'm targeting?
- Recent activity: would someone scrolling my feed feel confident I'm active and thoughtful in this space?

## QUALITY STANDARDS

- Never produce a message that could have been written by someone with a different background than mine
- Every message must contain at least one specific detail about the recipient, company, or role — not a slot left for me to fill in
- The ask must be one thing, clearly stated. Not "a call, or maybe just some advice, or a referral if you think it's appropriate"
- If my background is not a strong match for what I'm targeting, name that gap in the strategy section — do not paper over it with confident language
- Messages should feel like they were written by a smart professional with options, not someone who needs a job
